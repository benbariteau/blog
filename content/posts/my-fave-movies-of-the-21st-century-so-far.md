# 25) Abraham Lincoln: Vampire Hunter (2012)

Coming out swinging with this one, huh?
Well it's because this movie rules.
It's a silly premise, but it does it about as straight as it possibly can.
In particular, Benjamin Walker as Abraham Lincoln and Mary Elizabeth Winstead as Mary Todd Lincoln treat all of the non-supernatural scenes with the gravitas of an actual biopic.

And yet, we get some rollicking action.
A lot of it is low budget CGI, which somehow adds to the charm?
The geography and stakes of the action is always crystal clear so unlike a lot of supposedly "better" movies you're often at the edge of your seat.

On the flip side of our film's power couple we also have the scenery chewing of Dominic Cooper, Jimmi Simpson (my beautiful son), and Rufus Sewell.
Cooper is set to at least 8 in every scene.
Jimmi Simpson's Joshua Speed is a fairly functional character elevated by a riveting performance.
Sewell was frankly born to play this kind of mustache twirling villian, which he does to excllent effect.

Look, Abraham Lincoln uses an axe to kill vampires, what more could you ask for?

# 24) Hot Fuzz

The second and--by my measure--best of the Cornetto Triology brought to you by Edgar Wright, Simon Pegg, and Nick Forst.
Absolutely stacked cast in this including Timothy Dalton, Bill Nighy, Olivia "Won Best Actress" Colman, and Martin Freeman.
But the real star here--as it often is with these voices--is the writing.
Endless setups and payoffs in the middle of a sendup and/or love letter to the action genre.

# 23) Coherence

I'm kind of a sucker for zero-bugdet scifi.
It all works because it's built on a rock-solid character work which lets the twist turn the screws on their lives.
I don't really want to say more because not many have seen it so, I'll just say: check it out!

# 22) Casino Royale

I love me some campy Bond, but this one was a hell of a way to kick off the post-Bourne era.
Craig is immediately magnetic but also brutal instead of suave.
We also get just a whiff of the homoerotic tensions with the villian that blossom later on.

# 21) Get Out

Jordan Peele's debut hit us all like a truck.
A pointed exploration of being Black in America with just enough of a scifi twist to feel fresh.
It also uses just enough humor to keep us from completely falling into despair, but just right on the edge.
This makes it a pitch-perfect thriller.

# 20) Kingsman: The Secret Service

Hot Fuzz almost seems nice in comparison to this movie's sendup of James Bond.
But it is equally as reverant of a love letter as well.
Samuel L. Jackson as a murderous, yet also squemish tech CEO feels all too relevant today.
This is also the breakout of Taron Egerton AND Sofia Boutella.

The real achievement of this movie was cementing us in the post-John Wick era of movies.
Wick brought us crystal clear action when everyone else was still copying the Bourne shakycam.
Kingsman took that and make its own version of it, which still hasn't truly been topped for sheer complexity and effectiveness.

# 19) Sin City

Robert Rodriguez has always been a director who is in love with the pulp of yore.
